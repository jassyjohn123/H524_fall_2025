Anorexia Treatment Study

Statistical Analysis Report

Date: November 2, 2025

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive statistical analysis of a clinical
study comparing three treatment approaches for anorexia nervosa: Control
(no treatment), Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), and Family Therapy
(FT). The study examined weight changes in 72 participants across these
three treatment groups.

Key Findings

- Family Therapy showed the strongest results with a mean weight gain of
  7.26 lbs

- CBT demonstrated moderate effectiveness with a mean weight gain of
  3.01 lbs

- Control group showed a slight mean weight loss of -0.45 lbs

- Statistical analysis revealed significant differences between
  treatment groups (p = 0.0065)

- 76.5% of FT participants gained weight, compared to 62.1% in CBT and
  42.3% in Control

Dataset Overview

The dataset comprises weight measurements from 72 participants with
anorexia nervosa, distributed across three treatment groups. Each
participant's weight was recorded before (pre-treatment) and after
(post-treatment) the intervention period.

Statistical Analysis

One-Way ANOVA

A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted to compare weight
changes across the three treatment groups. The results revealed a
statistically significant difference between groups:

- F-statistic: 5.4223

- P-value: 0.0065 (p < 0.05)

This indicates that there is a statistically significant difference in
weight change between at least two of the treatment groups.

Pairwise Comparisons

Independent t-tests were conducted to identify which specific groups
differed from each other:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Comparison                      P-value              Significance
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Control vs CBT                  0.0996              Not Significant

  Control vs Family               0.0025          Significant (p < 0.05)
  Therapy                                         

  CBT vs Family Therapy           0.0612              Not Significant
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Key Finding: Family Therapy produced significantly greater weight gain
compared to the Control group (p = 0.0025). The differences between
Control vs CBT and CBT vs Family Therapy did not reach statistical
significance, although both showed trends in the expected direction.

Conclusions and Recommendations

Summary of Findings

This analysis of 72 participants across three treatment conditions
reveals important insights into the effectiveness of different
therapeutic approaches for anorexia nervosa:

- Family Therapy emerged as the most effective treatment, producing an
  average weight gain of 7.26 lbs with 76.5% of participants showing
  improvement. This difference was statistically significant when
  compared to the Control group (p = 0.0025).

- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy showed moderate effectiveness, with a
  mean weight gain of 3.01 lbs and 62.1% of participants gaining weight.
  While not statistically different from either group, CBT represents a
  middle ground between Control and FT.

- The Control group showed minimal improvement, with a slight average
  weight loss of -0.45 lbs, though with high variability (SD = 7.99).
  Only 42.3% of control participants gained weight.

Clinical Implications

- Prioritize Family Therapy: Given its superior outcomes and statistical
  significance, FT should be considered as a first-line treatment option
  for anorexia nervosa, particularly for younger patients where family
  involvement is feasible.

- CBT as Alternative: For cases where family therapy is not appropriate
  or available, CBT represents a viable alternative that produces
  positive outcomes for the majority of patients.

- Active Treatment Necessary: The Control group's poor outcomes
  underscore the importance of active therapeutic intervention rather
  than no treatment or monitoring alone.

Study Limitations and Future Research

- The Family Therapy group had a smaller sample size (n=17) compared to
  CBT (n=29) and Control (n=26), which may affect statistical power.

- Future studies should include longer follow-up periods to assess the
  durability of treatment effects.

- Investigation of moderating factors (e.g., age, illness duration,
  family dynamics) could help identify which patients benefit most from
  each treatment approach.

- Cost-effectiveness analysis would be valuable for treatment selection
  in resource-limited settings.
