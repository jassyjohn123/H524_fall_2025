=== SLIDE 5 ===
Independent and Dependent Events
Two events are independent of each other if knowing that one will occur (or has occurred) does not change the probability that the other occurs. 
Two events are dependent if knowing that one will occur (or has occurred) changes the probability that the other occurs.

=== SLIDE 6 ===
HOW TO DETERMINEIF TWO EVENTS ARE INDEPENDENT
1. Physical assumptionExample: Lottery draws on different days don’t affect each other. 
2. Pr(B|A) = P(B)?

If so, A and B are independent events, otherwise they are not. Example: Suppose data showed that smokers and non-smokers are equally likely to get the flu. 

P(flu | smoker) = P(flu) so they are independent. 

3. Events A and B are independent if and only if P(A and B) = P(A)P(B)

=== SLIDE 10 ===
Sensitivity and Specificity
Sensitivity: Probability of a positive test result given that the individual tested actually has the disease.
Specificity: Probability the results is negative given that the individual tested does not have the disease.

=== SLIDE 11 ===
Sensitivity and SpecificityPap Smear and Cervical Cancer
E.g. Overall, 16.25% of Pap tests performed on women with cancer resulted in false negative outcomes. 		Pr(test negative | cancer) = 0.1625So, sensitivity is:
		Pr(test positive | cancer) = 1 – 0.1625 = 0.8375
As it turns out, 18.64% of tests were false positives.
	Pr(test positive | no cancer) = 0.1864So, the specificity is:
	Pr(test negative | no cancer) = 1 – 0.1864 = 0.8136
