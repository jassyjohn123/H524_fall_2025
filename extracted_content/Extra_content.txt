Hypothesis Testing for Categorical and Ordinal Outcomes
2 or more sample: χ2 test for independence
Compare observed distribution of outcome response categories between treatment groups
One sample: χ2 goodness-of-fit test
Compare observed frequencies for each outcome category to expected outcomes for each category
χ2 tests are based on the agreement between expected (under H0) and observed (sample) frequencies

=== SLIDE 10 ===
Chi-Square Example and Interpretation
• Question of Interest: Is there a significant difference in the number of ACL tears between genders of 100 soccer players from a local university
• H0: ACL Tearsmales = ACL Tearsfemales
• Ha: ACL Tearsmales ≠ ACL Tearsfemales
• Chi-square value = 24.63
• Fail to reject if there is a difference in ACL tears between male and female soccer athletes (p = 0.620)

=== SLIDE 12 ===
Linear Regression: An Introduction
Goal:  relate a response (“outcome”, “dependent variable”) to one or more predictors (“covariates”, “independent variables”); 
In public health research, we are interested in looking at associations between variables
Statistically, we are trying to determine whether two variables are independent or dependent
A model of the relationship is hypothesized, and estimates of the parameter values are used to develop an estimated regression equation. 
If the model is deemed satisfactory, the estimated regression equation can be used to predict the value of the dependent variable given values for the independent variables

=== SLIDE 18 ===
Residuals and Least Sum of Squares
A residual is the difference between an observed value and the fitted value provided by a model. 
Ordinary least squares is a method for estimating the unknown parameters in a linear regression model. This method minimizes the sum of squared vertical distances between the observed responses and the predicted responses from the regression equation.

Simple Linear Regression: Example
Question of Interest: In a population of 100 individuals with cardiovascular disease: do individuals with a high BMI (predictor) have higher heart rates?





Model: yheartrate = β0 + β1(BMI) + e
On average, for every one unit increase in BMI, the average heart rate of individuals with CVD decreased by 0.01 beats per minute (p = 0.76)
