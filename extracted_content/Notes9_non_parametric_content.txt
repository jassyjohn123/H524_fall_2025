=== SLIDE 1 ===
Non-Parametric Methods

=== SLIDE 2 ===
We know that t-tests are robust to departures from Normality.
But how robust?
What do we do when normality cannot be assumed, and sample size is too small for CLT to hold?
Here we employ “nonparametric” methods
No specific distribution is assumed for the data
Less power and wider C.I.’s
Less flexible
One or two paired samples: Sign test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test
Two independent samples: Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test

=== SLIDE 4 ===
Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test
Close to the same power as paired t-test – often used as default.
Replace differences with ranks
Get differences for all pairs, D=X-Y
Ho: Distribution of D has median of 0
If no effect: observed d’s (observed differences) should be equally spread above and below 0
If y-group tends to give higher responses, majority of d’s should be < 0
If x-group tends to give higher responses, majority of d’s should be > 0

=== SLIDE 5 ===
Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test (cont.)
Calculate the magnitude of the differences |Di| and the ranks of these. The smallest value gets a rank of 1; the next smallest 2, and so on up to the max, which gets a rank of n.
 Get the “signed-ranks” by putting the signs of the Di’s (+ or -) onto the ranked list of the |Di|’s.
Test statistics: T is the sum of the ranks that have positive signs.
