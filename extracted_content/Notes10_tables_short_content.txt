Chi-squared Test
The χ2 test for independence evaluates whether or not there is an association between two categorical variables 
For example smoking status (nonsmoker, smoker, heavy smoker) and lung disease status (no disease, clinical signs of disease, disease) 
The Chi-Squared test for independence is a formal statistical procedure for determining whether or not an association suggested by an observed pattern in the data can be attributable to sampling variation alone.

=== SLIDE 4 ===
Properties of χ2 Distributions
Unlike t and Z distributions, but like F distributions, χ2 density curves are defined only on the positive real line 
Chi-squared distributions are not symmetric, they are right skewed.
Like all density curves, chi-squared curves never cross the x-axis and the total area under the curve is 1 
There are many chi-squared distributions. Each one is indexed by a single positive integer called degrees of freedom (df). Denoted either by df or m.

An r × c contingency table has r · c cells

=== SLIDE 9 ===
Reference Distribution
We need the expected counts and the reference distribution. 
The reference distribution is the distribution of chi-square statistic assuming that no association exists 
If in fact the two categorical variables are not associated, then χ2 comes from a distribution that for large sample size can be well-approximated by the χ2(r-1)(c-1) distribution 
So for 2 × 2 tables, the reference distribution is the χ2(2-1)(2-1)= χ21
