=== SLIDE 1 ===
Hypothesis Testing

=== SLIDE 2 ===
Null Hypothesis
The opposite of the scientific hypothesis, for example
	‘There is no effect of folic acid on neural tube defects’

Alternative (or Scientific) Hypothesis
This is what investigators are trying to prove.
	‘Folic acid helps prevent neural tube defects’

=== SLIDE 3 ===
Devise a quantity which measures the effect of interest (e.g. how much effect does taking folic acid have on the proportion of pregnancies resulting in neural tube defects).

This quantity is called the test statistic.

If the null hypothesis is true the parameter is “zero” (generally).

But with some uncertainty!

=== SLIDE 4 ===
Within the uncertainty, is the test statistic consistent with (typically) zero?

Must obtain the distribution of the test statistic under the null.
Spread in this distribution indicates the expected variability in the test statistic (if the experiment was repeated many times).

=== SLIDE 5 ===
P-value = P(TS is as extreme or more extreme than observed | null hypothesis).

Make a decision:  small p-value → reject null hypothesis.

=== SLIDE 6 ===
Procedures for Testing Statistical Hypotheses
Ask research question about population parameter
Translate question into two hypotheses
H0: Null Hypothesis -- hypothesis of “no difference” or “no effect”
Ha: Alternative Hypothesis -- hypothesis that reflects the scientific question 
one or two sided?
Pick significance level alpha,  (= 0.05)
Collect data

=== SLIDE 7 ===
Procedures for Testing Statistical Hypotheses (continued)
Calculate test statistic.
Convert test statistic to P-value (p).
If p < α, Reject H0 and accept Ha.  Statistically significant.
If p > α,  “Fail to Reject” H0.  Result is Non-significant (NS). (Controversy here.)
P-value is the probability of incorrectly rejecting H0 (Type I error).
“Acceptance” of H0 is a failure to reject H0 and does not mean that H0 is true (Type II error).

=== SLIDE 8 ===
Example 1: HIV(+) Patients
Research Question: Is the mean CD4 count for HIV(+) patients less than 400? Parameter = mean (µ); one sided alternative
H0: mean CD4 in HIV+ patients = 400
H0: µ = 400 -- can also be stated as µ≥ 400 
Ha: mean CD4 in HIV+ patients < 400 
Ha: µ < 400

=== SLIDE 9 ===
Example 2: BP Levels and Breakfast Cereals
Research Question: Is there a difference in mean BP levels between kids who eat oatmeal and kids who eat Froot Loops? Parameter = means (µ1,, µ2); two sided alternative
H0: µ1 (oatmeal) = µ2 (froot loops)
Ha: µ1 (oatmeal) ≠ µ2 (froot loops)

=== SLIDE 11 ===
Inference for a Population Mean
Assumptions: 
continuous variable X 
X is normally distributed with mean µ and 	variance σ2
Goal: compare mean µ to some known mean µ0
Design possibilities: 
one sample, variance known (large sample) (z-test)
one sample, variance unknown (assume normal population) (t-test)

=== SLIDE 12 ===
Inference for a Population Mean(continued)
Forms of hypotheses: 
H0: µ = µ0  
Ha: µ < µ0  (left-sided alternative)
Ha: µ > µ0  (right-sided alternative)
Ha: µ ≠ µ0 (two-sided alternative)

=== SLIDE 27 ===
Confidence Intervals are Like Two-Sided Hypothesis Tests
If the confidence interval does NOT cover hypothesized value, we reject Ho (null).
If the confidence interval does cover hypothesized value, we fail to reject Ho.
